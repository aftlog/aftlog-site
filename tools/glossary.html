Keeping your boat shipshape!

Glossary

Boat terms in plain talk — and the technical version for when it matters.

Impeller

Plain talk: The small rubber pump that moves cooling water through an outboard. Weak tell-tale usually means it's worn.

For the pro: The rubber water pump that pushes cooling water through the engine. If the “pee stream” (tell-tale) is weak, it's often the impeller. Replace every ~3 years or sooner if overheating.

Lower unit

Plain talk: The bottom gearcase of an outboard. Check its oil for milkiness (water) or metal flakes.

For the pro: The gearcase at the bottom of an outboard. Holds the gears and the drive shaft; contains gear oil that should be checked for a milky look (water intrusion).

Bellows

Plain talk: Rubber boots on a sterndrive that keep water out. Cracked bellows can sink a boat.

For the pro: The rubber accordion boots on a sterndrive that keep water out and grease in around the drive shaft and shift cable. Cracked bellows = sinking risk.

Gimbal bearing

Plain talk: A key bearing on a sterndrive that allows the drive to pivot. Water damage causes stiff steering.

For the pro: The bearing at the transom where a sterndrive pivots. Gets water-damaged if not greased; hard steering is a symptom.

Anode

Plain talk: A metal piece that corrodes on purpose to protect your engine or outdrive from corrosion.

For the pro: A sacrificial zinc (or magnesium/aluminum) bolt that corrodes instead of your outdrive. If half eaten, it's doing its job.

Tell-tale

Plain talk: The cooling-water stream from an outboard. Weak or no stream means pump trouble.

For the pro: The small “pee hole” stream of cooling water from an outboard. No stream = water pump problem.

Transom

Plain talk: The back wall of the boat where the engine mounts. Soft spots mean rot.

For the pro: The flat back of the boat the engine bolts to. Soft spots here mean rot — a major buying warning sign.

Stringers

Plain talk: Structural beams inside the hull. Rot leads to soft floors and major repairs.

For the pro: The internal framing that stiffens the hull. Rot here shows as soft floors.

Winterize

Plain talk: Protecting the boat for winter by draining water, treating fuel, and prepping the engine.

For the pro: Preparing the boat for freezing: drain water, fog the engine, stabilize fuel, remove the battery. Skipping it can crack the engine block.

Commissioning

Plain talk: Spring startup: charge battery, check impeller, inspect fuel, and run on muffs.

For the pro: Waking the boat up in spring: unwrap, charge the battery, check the impeller and fuel, first fire-up on muffs.

Porpoising

Plain talk: The bow oscillates up and down at speed. Usually caused by trim or engine height.

For the pro: The bow bouncing up and down on plane. Usually trim or motor-height related.

Prop slip

Plain talk: How much the prop loses grip in the water. Higher slip = less efficiency.

For the pro: The difference between theoretical and actual propeller speed — how efficiently the prop bites the water.

Bilge

Plain talk: The lowest part of the boat where water collects. The bilge pump removes it.

For the pro: The lowest inside part of the hull where water collects; the bilge pump removes it.

Muffs

Plain talk: Rubber cups that feed water to an outboard for running it on land.

For the pro: The clamp-on ear muffs that feed water to an outboard's cooling system while running it on land.

Float plan

Plain talk: A note telling someone your route and return time. Huge safety benefit.

For the pro: Who you tell where you're going and when you'll be back — the single best safety habit.

Gear lube

Plain talk: Oil inside the lower unit. Milky means water; metal means wear.

For the pro: The oil in the lower unit. Milky = water got in. Metal flakes = gears are wearing. Both are bad signs when buying.

HIN

Plain talk: The boat's 12-digit serial number. Must match registration.

For the pro: Hull Identification Number — the boat's 12-character serial, like a VIN. Found on the transom; verify it matches the registration.

Osmosis

Plain talk: Water blisters under the gelcoat. Cosmetic or structural depending on depth.

For the pro: Blisters under the gelcoat from water absorbed into the hull. Common on older fiberglass — can be cosmetic or serious.

Skeg

Plain talk: The fin under the prop. Damage usually means an impact.

For the pro: The fin below the propeller that protects the prop and helps steering. Bent or broken = a past impact.

Rub rail

Plain talk: The bumper strip around the hull. Damage shows docking impacts.

For the pro: The rubber/plastic bumper strip around the hull's top edge. Separated or cracked = dock or impact history.

Splashwell

Plain talk: A recessed area near the outboard that helps keep water out.

For the pro: The recessed area at the back of the boat around the outboard that keeps following water out.

Primer bulb

Plain talk: A rubber bulb that primes fuel to the engine. Should feel firm.

For the pro: The squeezable rubber bulb in the fuel line used to prime the engine. Should feel firm; soft or cracked = suspect.

Water-separating filter

Plain talk: A fuel filter that removes water. Replace yearly.

For the pro: The fuel filter that catches water before it reaches the engine. Dirty = poor fuel maintenance.

Livewell

Plain talk: A tank with pumps to keep fish alive.

For the pro: The tank that keeps caught fish alive — has its own pumps and plumbing.

Compression test

Plain talk: A test of cylinder pressure. Big differences mean engine issues.

For the pro: Measures each cylinder's pressure; all should be within 10% of each other. A big difference = expensive engine trouble.

Keel

Plain talk: The centerline ridge on the bottom of the hull. Often scraped or worn.

For the pro: The bottom ridge of the hull that takes damage when the boat is beached.

Chines

Plain talk: The edges where the hull bottom meets the sides. Important for stability.

For the pro: The hull edges where the bottom meets the sides.

Freeboard

Plain talk: How tall the boat's sides are above the water. More freeboard = safer in waves.

For the pro: The height of the hull side above the waterline.

Gunwale

Plain talk: The top edge of the boat's sides. Often used as a handhold.

For the pro: The top edge or rail of the hull sides.

Trim tab

Plain talk: A small plate that helps keep the boat level.

For the pro: Adjustable plates on the hull or outdrive that correct listing and optimize running attitude.

Cavitation

Plain talk: Air bubbles around the prop that cause vibration and lost power.

For the pro: The collapse of vapor bubbles around the propeller blades, causing vibration and thrust loss.

Planing

Plain talk: When the boat rises and skims on top of the water.

For the pro: The hydrodynamic state where the hull rises and rides on top of the water, reducing drag.

Draft

Plain talk: How deep the boat sits in the water.

For the pro: The vertical distance between the waterline and the lowest point of the hull or drive.

Beam

Plain talk: The width of the boat at its widest point.

For the pro: The maximum width of the vessel, influencing stability and interior volume.

Deadrise

Plain talk: How V-shaped the hull is — deeper V = smoother ride.

For the pro: The angle between the hull bottom and a horizontal plane; higher angles improve rough-water performance.

Chine walk

Plain talk: A side-to-side wobble at high speed.

For the pro: Instability at high speed where the hull oscillates between chines due to trim or weight imbalance.

LOA (Length Overall)

Plain talk: The boat's full length from front to back.

For the pro: Total vessel length including appendages such as swim platforms and bow pulpits.

LWL (Waterline Length)

Plain talk: How much of the boat sits in the water.

For the pro: The length of the hull at the waterline, affecting hull speed and stability.

Trim angle

Plain talk: How high or low the motor is tilted.

For the pro: The angular position of the outboard or sterndrive relative to the transom, affecting lift and efficiency.

Heel

Plain talk: When the boat leans to one side.

For the pro: Temporary lateral inclination caused by wind, waves, or turning forces.

List

Plain talk: A steady lean caused by uneven weight.

For the pro: Persistent lateral inclination caused by uneven weight distribution.

Pitch

Plain talk: The bow moving up and down.

For the pro: Longitudinal motion of the bow and stern in waves.

Yaw

Plain talk: The bow swinging left and right.

For the pro: Rotational motion of the bow swinging port-starboard around the vertical axis.

Roll

Plain talk: Side-to-side rocking.

For the pro: Side-to-side rotational motion around the vessel's longitudinal axis.

Scupper

Plain talk: Deck drains that let water flow out.

For the pro: Deck drainage openings that discharge water overboard; essential for self-bailing systems.

Self-bailing cockpit

Plain talk: A cockpit that drains water overboard instead of into the bilge.

For the pro: A cockpit designed to drain water via scuppers without relying on bilge pumps.

Hull speed

Plain talk: The fastest a displacement hull can go efficiently.

For the pro: The theoretical maximum efficient speed of a displacement hull, based on waterline length.

Displacement hull

Plain talk: A hull that pushes through the water instead of riding on top.

For the pro: A hull form that moves through the water without planing, prioritizing stability over speed.

Planing hull

Plain talk: A hull that lifts and skims on top of the water.

For the pro: A hull designed to generate lift and ride above the water at higher speeds.

Ventilation (prop)

Plain talk: When air reaches the prop and causes a sudden loss of thrust.

For the pro: Ingress of air to the propeller blades, causing RPM flare and loss of bite.

Following seas

Plain talk: Waves coming from behind the boat.

For the pro: Sea conditions where waves approach from astern, affecting steering and stability.

Quartering seas

Plain talk: Waves hitting the boat at an angle.

For the pro: Waves approaching at an oblique angle, influencing yaw and roll behavior.

Backing down (anchoring)

Plain talk: Reversing gently to set the anchor.

For the pro: Applying reverse thrust to set the anchor firmly into the seabed.

Transom angle

Plain talk: The angle of the back of the boat where the engine mounts.

For the pro: The geometric angle of the transom, influencing engine mounting height and trim range.

Keel guard

Plain talk: A strip that protects the keel from scrapes.

For the pro: A sacrificial protective strip installed on the keel to prevent abrasion during beaching or loading.

Fairlead

Plain talk: A guide that keeps a rope or anchor line from rubbing.

For the pro: A roller or guide that directs a line to prevent chafe and maintain proper lead angle.
